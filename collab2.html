Member Information Cards

What each member brings to the table.

The guidance, frameworks, and training each founding initiative contributes.

OWASP AI Exchange
owaspai.org
OWASP AI Exchange provides 300+ pages of free, evolving, practitioner-focused guidance for securing AI systems, including AI threats, controls, risk analysis, privacy, testing, and development/runtime security. It is an open-source global collaboration that connects AI-security experts, industry, researchers, and policymakers to help shape standards and align with frameworks such as the EU AI Act and ISO standards.
SANS Institute
sans.org
SANS provides hands-on AI security training, including courses on securing GenAI/LLM applications, AI/ML for cybersecurity, cloud AI security, and related GIAC certifications such as GAIPS and GMLE. It also offers practitioner resources — webinars, research, tools, and maturity/governance guidance — to help teams defend AI systems and use AI responsibly in security operations, incident response, and threat detection.
OWASP GenAI Security Project
genai.owasp.org
OWASP GenAI LLM Top 10 2026, OWASP Top 10 for Agentic Applications for 2026, OWASP GenAI Data Security Risks & Mitigations 2026, OWASP AIBOM Generator.
Berryville Institute of Machine Learning
berryvilleiml.com
BIML is a non-profit science and technology organization whose research focuses on machine learning security.
Center for Internet Security
cisecurity.org
CIS Hardened Images, CIS Controls, CIS Benchmarks, CIS SecureSuite.
Cloud Security Alliance
cloudsecurityalliance.org
CSA Research is created by the industry for the industry and is both vendor-neutral and consensus driven. CSA Working Groups are the go-to source for best practices, research and tools for providing security assurance and privacy in the cloud.
Coalition for Secure AI
coalitionforsecureai.org
The Coalition for Secure AI (CoSAI) is an open ecosystem of AI and security experts from industry leading organizations. CoSAI workstreams: Software Supply Chain Security for AI Systems, AI Security Risk Governance, Secure Design Patterns for Agentic Systems.
National Institute of Standards and Technology
nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework
The NIST AI Risk Management Framework (AI RMF) is intended for voluntary use and to improve the ability to incorporate trustworthiness considerations into the design, development, use, and evaluation of AI products, services, and systems.
